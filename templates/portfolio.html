Value Score (0 – 100)

A composite score that rates a stock's attractiveness as a value investment. Each metric below is scored green (100), yellow (50), or red (0) against historical benchmarks, then combined using category weights.

70 – 100 Strong value candidate. Most fundamentals look attractive.
40 – 69 Mixed signals. Some strengths, some weaknesses.
0 – 39 Expensive or weak fundamentals. Proceed with caution.

The score is a starting point, not a buy/sell signal. A low score for a high-growth tech stock may be normal — growth stocks trade at premium valuations by design.

Valuation (35% of score)

Measures how expensive the stock is relative to its earnings, assets, and cash flow. Lower is generally cheaper.

P/E (Forward)Same as trailing P/E but uses analyst estimates for next year's earnings.< 1212 – 20> 20

Tips for Interpreting

  • Compare within sectors. A P/E of 30 is expensive for a bank but normal for a tech company. Always compare similar businesses.
  • No single metric tells the whole story. A cheap P/E with terrible margins might be a value trap. Look at the full picture.
  • Growth vs. value. High-growth companies (NVDA, AMZN) will almost always score lower — that doesn't mean they're bad investments.
  • Use the Compare tab. Side-by-side comparison of two stocks in the same sector is more insightful than absolute numbers.
